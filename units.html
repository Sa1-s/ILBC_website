Five connected units

Explore the faith: how the Church receives it, teaches it, celebrates it, and lives it.

How every unit begins“I love being Catholic because of…”

…and explores one part of the faith in relationship to the whole.

Together, the units help participants understand how the Church receives the faith, teaches it, celebrates it, and lives it.

Unit 1 of 5

The Magisterium and the Deposit of Faith

How does the Church receive and hand on the faith?

This unit introduces the deposit of faith entrusted to the Church and the Magisterium, her teaching office. Participants explore how Scripture and Sacred Tradition transmit the one apostolic faith, and how the Magisterium serves the Word of God.

What participants exploreThe relationship among Scripture, Tradition, and the Church’s teaching, and why it matters when reading the Bible, celebrating the sacraments, or considering a Catholic practice.
Unit 2 of 5

The Catechism as a Guide to the Faith

The Catechism of the Catholic Church offers a coherent account of what Catholics believe, celebrate, live, and pray.

This unit helps participants become comfortable using it as a guide, following a question from its biblical foundations to the Church’s teaching and its place in daily life.

What participants exploreHow to find a teaching, read it in context, and recognize its connections with worship, moral life, and prayer.
Unit 3 of 5

Sacred Scripture Whole and Entire

The Bible is received and read within the Church’s living Tradition.

This unit introduces ways of reading inspired Scripture that attend to literary form, historical setting, the unity of the Bible, and the coherence of faith. A passage heard at Mass becomes an invitation to discover its wider meaning.

What participants exploreHow to read a passage in context, recognize the limits of an isolated quotation, and connect the Word proclaimed in the liturgy with doctrine and sacramental life.
Unit 4 of 5

The Sacraments as Encounters with Christ

The seven sacraments are encounters with Jesus Christ, who acts through these effective signs of grace.

This unit explores their signs and words and the place of each sacrament in Catholic life. Speaking of Christ as “the Sacrament” points to him as their source, not to an eighth sacrament.

What participants exploreThe seven sacraments, their relationship to Christ’s action, and the connections among Scripture, the Church’s teaching, sacramental celebration, and daily discipleship.
Unit 5 of 5

The Smells and the Bells

Catholic life engages the senses through prayer, ritual, disciplines, and customs.

This unit explores liturgy, sacramentals, devotions, the Church year, and local practices. Participants consider how these expressions of faith nourish prayer and lead toward charity and everyday discipleship.

What participants exploreThe distinction among a sacrament, a sacramental, and a devotion, and the difference between the Church’s shared faith and customs that can vary across times and places.

The sign of the cross
A glimpse of the experience

Begin with a familiar gesture

Consider the sign of the cross. A gathering could begin with this familiar gesture and explore its scriptural and baptismal setting. Participants could connect it with the Church’s profession of faith, then reflect on what it means to pray with the body during an ordinary day. One simple practice would open a conversation that reaches across the program’s themes.

A gathering can include:

  1. Prayer and Scripture
  2. A short teaching
  3. Guided conversation
  4. Practical application
  5. A closing reflection
60–75 minutes — a useful starting point for planning, rather than a fixed requirement.

The pace should leave room for understanding and participation and can be adjusted to the setting.
